Procedural India

86 KB of skeleton. Everything else is arithmetic.

You cannot procedurally generate India — noise gives you a landmass, not this one. So the split is: the shape is data, the detail is code. A 132-ring coastline and 103 orographic control points go in; terrain, rainfall, biomes and shading come out of a function. No PNG, no mesh, no baked art.

01

The map, running

Drag to pan, scroll to zoom. Every pixel below is computed from the skeleton at the moment you look at it — nothing here is an image. Step through the layers to see it assemble.

Layer —
1 · Rasterise

132 coastline rings are filled to a mask. Exact where the data is exact — no noise touches the outline.

2 · Coast field

A two-pass chamfer distance transform. Every land pixel learns how far it is from the sea.

3 · Height

15 ridges, 7 swells, 7 river troughs. Gaussian falloff, ridged noise on top, troughs carved after.

4 · Rainfall

Air marches in from the south-west and drops moisture wherever the ground rises. The Thar and the Deccan shadow emerge — nobody painted them.

5 · Shade & tint

Hillshade from the height gradient using the locked rig, then a biome ramp keyed on height, rainfall and latitude.

400× smaller than Victoria 3's map, and it never blurs.

Because there is no resolution to run out of. Down to ~660 m the coastline is Natural Earth's real geometry; below that, deterministic noise adds detail that is plausible rather than surveyed — which is exactly the boundary India's Geospatial Data Guidelines 2021 draw at one metre anyway. The legal line and the technical line are the same line.

03

Progressive, three ways

Within a frame

Renders at ⅛ scale first, then ¼, ½, 1:1. You see the map immediately and it sharpens. Drag and it drops back to ⅛ so panning stays fluid.

Across zoom

Five LODs, 0.8 KB to 61 KB. The renderer picks by scale; below LOD 4 procedural detail takes over and never runs out.

Across the project

Terrain now, rivers and settlements next, then the political layer as its own SOI-compliant vector set. The base map never gets rebuilt — layers get added to it.

The generator is a pure function of (lon, lat, seed). Same inputs, same terrain, on every machine, forever — which is the same determinism requirement the simulation core already has, so tiles seam and multiplayer agrees without syncing a single pixel.

Coastline, rivers and lakes: Natural Earth 10m, public domain, clipped to 66–99.5°E / 5–38°N. Land polygons, not country polygons — the base map depicts no international boundary at all, which keeps it clear of the Criminal Law (Amendment) Act 1990 issue; political borders arrive later as a separate Survey-of-India-compliant layer. Orography is 103 authored control points, not a sampled DEM. Lighting follows the locked rig: warm key, upper-left, 35°.
