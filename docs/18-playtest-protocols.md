# First Contact — the playtest protocols and the pre-human baselines

**Phase 43.** Instrumentation is in the client (the `metrics` button — local
only, copied to the clipboard, nothing leaves the machine on its own), the
two protocols below are ready to hand to a tester, and each carries the
headless baseline the human numbers will be compared against. The
pre-commitment from `16-gap-closure.md` stands: **the cadence table moves on
evidence, and the 210 hours are not sacred.**

Three testers minimum, one of whom has never heard the pitch. The deliverable
of the human round is this document's §4, filled in.

---

## 1. What the client records

Wall time per era · decisions per era · cards and year pages opened · cards
kept · threads opened · pauses · losses noticed (risk panel opened within 20
in-game years of a corpus loss) vs losses total. Exported as JSON by the
`metrics` button. No network, no identifiers.

## 2. Protocol A — the Chola slice (two hours)

The phase-34 ruling's window: **985–1070**, Rajaraja's accession to
Kulottunga's union. Full-system: survey, temple economy, corpus, trade,
the fleet, sovereignty, named people.

**Script for the tester:** start the Chola campaign; play to 1070 or two
hours, whichever first. No other instructions — what a tester does *unprompted*
is the data.

**Prompts for the debrief (ask, don't lead):**
1. When were you bored, and what were you waiting for?
2. What did you believe the game wanted you to do? When did you learn it?
3. Which card did you read fully, and which did you start skipping? Why?
4. Did you notice anything was at risk before it was lost?
5. What would you do differently on a second run? (The wish to have one is
   itself a metric.)

**Headless baseline (seed `chola-slice`, engaged-player script):**

| Measure | Baseline |
|---|---|
| Slice length at cadence | 214 play-minutes |
| Decisions applied | 77 — one per **2.8 minutes** |
| Log lines (events + texture + trade) | 154 |
| Worst silent stretch | 7.6 minutes |
| Corpus at 1070 | 54 extant · 13 lost · 32 at risk |

The slice ruling holds on the numbers: the Chola window sustains a decision
every ~3 minutes with every system engaged. A human below half that rate is
a finding about discoverability, not density.

## 3. Protocol B — the Indus session (ninety minutes)

**2600–1900 BCE.** The no-villain era. Same rule: no instructions beyond
"survive it however seems right".

**Debrief prompts:**
1. Did you understand nobody was coming to fight you? When?
2. What did you do about the drying, and did the game seem to notice?
3. Did you try to save the cities? How did it feel when you couldn't?
4. Was the quiet register (texture incidents, the emptying notices)
   atmospheric or dead?

**Headless baseline (docs/17-indus-session.md):** decision density one per
**18.5 minutes** — the known miss against the one-per-five target, with the
three candidate remedies (more era verbs · fewer era hours · accept the
register) waiting on exactly this protocol's human data.
