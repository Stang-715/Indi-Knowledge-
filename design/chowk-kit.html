A scheduler nobody is measured against becomes a form people fill in afterwards. Publishing promised-versus-actual per department, permanently, is what converts 4.2 from paperwork into a commitment — and it costs nothing to build, because the data is already there the moment 4.4 issues a permit.

02 — Brand kit

Colour taken from the street, not from a trend deck

Each surface owns one hue, and every hue is lifted from something physical in Indian public life. That gives the app four instantly distinguishable rooms without four unrelated palettes — and it means the colour is explainable, which is how it survives a redesign.

Surface 1
Marigold#E8991F

The garland flower — hung at every doorway, gate and portrait. Sarathi's colour: warm, present, human.

Surface 2
Harbour#0E7C86

Deep-water green of the container ports. Belongs to trade, export tonnage and the map.

Surface 3
Indelible#6B4EA8

The violet of election ink on a finger. No colour says franchise faster in this country.

Surface 4
Milestone#1F6B4A

The green half of a highway kilometre stone. Roads, works, distance still to cover.

Ground
Indigo Ink#0F1320

Not black — pulled toward indigo, the dye this country exported for three centuries. Glass needs a ground with a bias.

Signal
Alert#D6452F

Reserved. Overrun works, closed roads, a bill closing in hours. Never decorative.

The one restraint on this palette

Marigold and Milestone sit near saffron and green, so they are never used adjacent at full saturation, never stacked as horizontal bands, and never paired with white between them. The app is civic, not national — it must not read as anybody's flag or anybody's party.

Why Anek. It is one superfamily covering Latin plus ten Indic scripts on a shared skeleton, with a width axis — so the big condensed numerals and a Malayalam headline come from the same typeface, at the same weight, with no visual seam. On a platform that has to speak twelve languages, that is a structural choice, not a stylistic one.

Rationale
Motion

One easing curve across the whole system — cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1), the iOS spring feel. Islands morph in 480 ms, disclosures open in 420 ms, taps scale to .96. Nothing fades in on scroll; nothing moves that isn't responding to a touch.

Voice

Plain, specific, and never congratulatory. "Water off 14–16 March" — not "Important service update". Sarathi is allowed to say the uncomfortable thing: how few people answered a poll, or that a pseudonym is not armour.

Iconography

1.75 px stroke, round caps, 24 px grid, drawn not borrowed. No emoji anywhere in the interface — they carry a tone this subject cannot afford and render differently on every handset in the market.

03 — UI kit

Every component, live. Press them.

These are not pictures of components — they are the components, running. What you tap here is what gets lifted into the build, which is the only way a kit stays honest once code starts.

Buttonsbtn

Pill radius throughout, 46 px minimum target, 56 px for the primary action on a screen. Only one hue-filled button per view — if two things look primary, neither is.

Glass actionbtn-glass

Glass only ever sits over content — a map, the caricature stage, a photograph. Over a flat panel it has nothing to refract and just looks like a weak grey.

Segmented controlseg

The thumb slides rather than cuts, so the eye follows which option it landed on. Two to four options only; beyond that it becomes a select.

05 — Before I build

What carries over, and what I need from you

Already built and working

Roughly half of surface 1 and surface 3 exist in the repo today. Sarathi is running — the caricature, the mood system, the ~30-topic knowledge base, speech. The advisory-vote flow, the balanced debate ordering, the aggregation layer with coverage figures and the append-only audit trail are all live. They get re-skinned into this kit, not rewritten.

The one place this spec pushes against the existing rules

Surface 2 puts stores on a public map. The build has a hard constraint that no device location is ever read — so a store pin is a stated business address, typed by the owner, exactly like the locality field. The map shows shops, never shoppers. I will keep it that way unless you tell me otherwise, because the constraint is enforced by a check that fails the build.

Six decisions I need before code

1 Is “Chowk” the name?

The crossroads where a town gathers, trades, argues and — literally — where the roads get dug. It maps to all four surfaces and it is not a government word, which matters: the app must never look like it speaks for the state. Say the word and I will rename everything.

2 Where does the live data actually come from?

Weather is straightforward — IMD or a commercial API. Agriculture and export volumes are harder: APEDA and DGCIS publish, but on a lag and in shapes that need cleaning. Do you have a data source or budget in mind, or should I build against published open data and show the lag honestly on each figure?

3 Who verifies a government department on surface 4?

This is the hardest problem in the whole app and it is not a design problem. A scheduler only means something if a fake “Water Board” cannot file a permit. Is there an existing departmental identity system to hook into, or is the first version an invite-and-vouch list you administer by hand?

4 Does the store listing get moderated before it appears?

Un-moderated, the map fills with junk in a week. Moderated, someone has to do the work. My recommendation is auto-publish with a report button and a verified tier for businesses that submit a GST number — visible difference, no queue.

5 Bills — Parliament only, or state assemblies too?

You said parliamentary, but you also said state-by-state. Assemblies are where most things that affect a ward actually get decided, and the data is far messier. I would ship Parliament first and add assemblies state by state.

6 Do you want a backend now, or another local build?

Everything so far runs on the device with a mock data layer shaped like the future API. Stores on a shared public map and departmental scheduling both genuinely need a server — that is the point where this stops being a front-end project.

My recommendation on sequencing

Build surfaces 1 and 3 to completion first — they are half-done, they need no server, and they are the two that prove the product. Surface 4 next, because a scheduler is valuable even with ten departments and it is the most original thing here. Surface 2 last: it is the one that depends most on data deals and a backend, and it is the easiest to get wrong quietly.
